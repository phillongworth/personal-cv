Kirklees Health and Wellbeing Strategy

Senior Manager, Kirklees Council · 2017 – 2023

Overview

The Kirklees Health and Wellbeing Strategy is the shared plan for how the Council, the NHS, Healthwatch, the voluntary sector and other partners work together to improve health and wellbeing for people of all ages who live, work or study in Kirklees. It sets out the vision, values, ways of working and priorities for action, with more detailed plans sitting underneath it.

I led the initial development and implementation of the strategy, and then its revision and update in 2023.

What the strategy is about

The 2022–2027 strategy has three priorities:

  • Mental wellbeing: helping everyone have good mental wellbeing and quality of life, with more emphasis on prevention and recovery and treating mental and physical ill-health equally
  • Healthy places: making the healthy choice the easy choice, through things like good planning, safe active travel and cleaner air
  • Connected care and support: making sure people get the right help when they need it, with joined-up services and without having to repeat their story

What changed in the update

Kirklees had a health and wellbeing strategy in place since 2014. The update started with a review of how that strategy had done against its original outcomes. Many of its ideas still held up, including prevention, tackling inequalities, personalised care, building on community strengths and supporting unpaid carers.

But a lot had changed. The Covid pandemic, the cost of living crisis, the climate emergency and changes to how local health and care partnerships are organised all needed to be reflected.

Where the evidence came from

  • The Kirklees Joint Strategic Assessment: the shared analysis of local health and care needs
  • The Director of Public Health's annual reports
  • The 2021 Currently Living in Kirklees survey, with more than 6,000 residents responding
  • Place Standard feedback and Healthwatch Kirklees surveys from 2021 and 2022

Healthwatch Kirklees turned what people said into "I statements" describing what good care should look and feel like.

How it's governed

The Health and Wellbeing Board has overall responsibility for the strategy and holds partners to account for delivering it. Progress is tracked through a shared outcomes framework, with headline indicators reviewed each year alongside the Joint Strategic Assessment.

What made it difficult

Strategies like this have no single line of command behind them. Delivery depends on organisations with their own boards, budgets and statutory duties choosing to line up, so the work is as much about building partnerships and governance that last as it is about the document itself.

The period also spanned the pandemic, during which I led the West Yorkshire PPE response and the Covid vaccination programme for social care staff in Kirklees.
